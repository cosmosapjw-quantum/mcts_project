## Proof details and convergence disposition

For the registered ideal categorical model, let F_t(h,a) be the deterministic state update including any reserved selections, leaf evaluations and committed backup marks appropriate to that clock. Conditional on fixed initial root and evaluator, define K_t(h,dh',da,dr) = p_t(a|h) delta_{F_t(h,a)}(dh',dr) (with a deterministic staged update before marks are delivered). The kernel has unit mass because the root categorical probabilities sum to one; repeated integration gives a normalized measure on complete histories. A path's projected action sequence identifies its marked history when F_t is deterministic; if not, the downstream conditional kernel must be retained. Under the actual PRNG implementation a seed determines the stream, so this ideal measure is a model of the randomized algorithm, not a claim that finitely many seeds produce every history. Recorded raw p and actual ordered choices authenticate the statistical action at each sampled history. The original -log N(s,a) has +1/N^2 second derivative in independent positive N coordinates, not the original proposed negative diagonal; discrete path variation is not defined without a chart. An argmax-count path is not generally the argmax-PUCT path, since U_a=Q_a+c P_a sqrt(n_parent)/(1+n_a) is not monotone increasing in N_a. Observed positive mass on final-unvisited actions is an independent obstruction to using -log N as the full-support action.

For a fixed finite law P with strictly positive weights on its support, W(J)=log sum_x P(x) exp(J dot X(x)). The first and second derivatives are phi=E_J X and H=Cov_J X. A vector v has v^T H v=0 iff v dot X is constant on support; on the affine tangent coordinate space H is positive definite. The Legendre-Fenchel supremum at phi=grad W(J) is attained at J by convexity; strict convexity there gives uniqueness and the inverse function theorem yields Hess Gamma=H^{-1}. For any q supported on P, D(q||P_J)=D(q||P)-J dot E_q X+W(J)>=0, with equality iff q=P_J. Hence Gamma(phi)=min_{E_q X=phi} D(q||P)=D(P_J||P) at interior image; singular boundary/exterior handled by convex conjugate, not by Hessian inversion. This result does not require an independent-wave approximation.

For Y on an integer lattice, fixed law and compact set of real tilts s with positive-definite H_s, Fourier inversion gives tilted local mass:
p_s^{*M}(k)=(2pi)^(-2) integral_{[-pi,pi]^2} [E_s exp(i t dot (Y-k/M))]^M dt,
where mu_s=k/M. The centered characteristic function has the same modulus as the uncentered one. If the differences of support generate Z^2, modulus one requires t dot (y-y') in 2pi Z for all differences, hence t=0 modulo the reciprocal lattice. Compactness makes the supremum outside any neighborhood of this point strictly below one, uniformly in s. Near zero the analytic logarithm is -t^T H_s t/2 - i kappa3[t^3]/6 + kappa4[t^4]/24 + terms of order |t|^5. Set t=z/sqrt(M). Terms of order M^-1/2 and M^-3/2 are odd polynomials and integrate to zero over a symmetric local region. At order M^-1 the terms are kappa4[z^4]/24-kappa3[z^3]^2/72. H_s has a uniform positive smallest eigenvalue on the compact set, and bounded support yields uniform bounded Taylor derivatives to any fixed order. For example split the local integral at |z|=M^(1/20), expand the logarithm through degree 5 with O(M^-2 |z|^6) remainder and expand its exponential through M^-3/2; the integrated error is O(M^-2) against a Gaussian times a fixed polynomial. For M^(1/20)<|z|<delta sqrt(M), the local Gaussian envelope gives a tail smaller than any inverse power of M. The external integral is exponentially small. Gaussian integration gives p_s^{*M}(k)=(2pi M sqrt(det H_s))^-1(1+C_s/M+O(M^-2)), uniformly for saddle tilts in that compact set. Untilt by exp(M K(s)-s dot k). This is a fixed-law asymptotic, not a statement uniform in changing board size, empirical sample size, vanishing support weight or approaching boundary. The observed M^2 scaled errors are finite diagnostics, not a rigorous universal constant.
