## Derivations, implementation and completed scope
On the finite population of roots x with normalized weight pi_x, define P_pi(dx,dgamma)=pi_x P_x(dgamma), Z_x(J)=E_x exp(J dot X), W_x=log Z_x. Summing first over complete paths gives Z_pi=sum_x pi_x Z_x. For pi_J=pi_x Z_x/Z_pi, differentiation gives grad W_pi=sum pi_J grad W_x. Since grad pi_J=pi_J(grad W_x-grad W_pi), one more derivative gives Hess W_pi=sum pi_J Hess W_x+sum pi_J (grad W_x-grad W_pi)(grad W_x-grad W_pi)^T. Both terms PSD, and the second is population heterogeneity, not intrinsic dynamics. Jensen W_pi>=sum pi_x W_x; equality iff W_x constant on roots of positive weight. The same identity applies to empirical finite donor laws. A two-component point mass example gives W''=p_J(1-p_J)(a-b)^2 although both within-root Hessians vanish. Wolfram explicit derivatives returned zero residuals for grad and Hess. This explains why pooling across root geometries can produce apparent fluctuation and why unconditional action is not a geometry-conditioned effective action. In empirical coordinate X=(fraction, signed mean), the variance decomposition has no physical thermal identification. Rescaling mark to (mark+1)/2 changes trace fractions; per-coordinate fractions reported.
